Create a 1600 x 900 pixel, 16:9 editorial hero illustration for AI Tamer.

STYLE: Editorial art that feels handmade from cut, torn and layered paper.
Use tactile paper grain, slightly imperfect cut edges, overlapping matte
surfaces and gentle shadows between layers. The result should feel warm,
crafted and expressive, with recognizable shapes and a clear visual metaphor.
Avoid glossy surfaces, plastic-looking objects and polished 3D rendering.

COLOR PROFILE: Muted, earthy, ink-and-paper colors. Acceptable colors include
slate blue, steel blue, dusty blue, blue-grey, muted teal, deep navy, warm cream,
ivory, soft sand, rusty reds, sulfur yellows and coral. Choose a harmonious
subset that suits the story rather than using every color or repeating one
fixed background. Balance cool blues with warm paper tones and restrained
rust, yellow or coral accents. Colors should feel softly weathered and
pigmented, not neon, oversaturated or fluorescent. Keep enough contrast for
the subject to read clearly at article-card size.

BACKGROUND: Enrich the chosen background lightly with fine paper grain,
faint overlapping paper layers in related tones, and one or two low-contrast
environmental details grounded in the article's metaphor. Keep these details
smaller, softer and quieter than the main subject. Preserve generous negative
space. Avoid unrelated floating objects, busy patterns and background elements
that compete with the focal subject. Maintain clear subject/background
contrast rather than applying one identical palette to every article.

COMPOSITION: One clear article-specific visual metaphor, supported by at most
two secondary elements. Make the relationship between objects visually clear.
Use recognizable subject silhouettes, not arbitrary geometric approximations.
Do not add unrelated floating shapes or decoration. Keep important objects
away from outer edges so cropping preserves the idea. The image must remain
legible at a small article-card size. Use depth through paper layering rather
than glossy lighting. Avoid generic robot, brain or network wallpaper.

SITE MARK: For newly created images, add the exact text © https://aitamer.news
at the bottom-right in small, readable type (about 18–22 pixels at 1600 x 900),
inset about 24 pixels from both edges. Use subtle contrasting ink so the mark
is legible without competing with the subject. This site mark is the only
permitted text or watermark. Existing published images do not need restamping.

EXCLUDE: All other text, letters, numbers, captions, labels, watermarks, signatures,
company logos, product UI screenshots, flags and recognizable people. No
photorealism, glossy 3D rendering, neon cyberpunk effects, busy circuitry or
dramatic gradients. Do not depict a vendor feature as tested or proven when
the article only reports an announcement.

OUTPUT: Clean illustration with only the small site mark. The final upload must be a
genuine JPEG exactly 1600 x 900 pixels. Inspect the actual image before upload;
check the exact © symbol, URL, placement and readability, and regenerate if
it violates these requirements. Automated file checks do not
approve visual style.

The caller appends SUBJECT: with one truthful article-specific metaphor,
identifying the main object and its relation to any supporting objects.

======================================================================================
CALLER INSTRUCTIONS. Send NOTHING from this line down to the image model.
======================================================================================

Two supported ways to get the site mark into a hero. The ordered steps for both, and
what is shared (one mark only, exactly 1600 x 900, keep the original, upload the
final file only), are written once in docs/guides/hero-procedure.md. Follow it.

PATH A, the model draws the mark in the same generation (Codex imagegen and any
model that can do it reliably): send everything above this line, as it stands, with
your SUBJECT line. The SITE MARK paragraph is the mark's specification.

PATH B, a deterministic stamp (the Grok bots, Claude, Cursor, anyone whose model
cannot draw the mark reliably, and the fallback for path A): the model must draw NO
lettering and must never see the site address. Send everything above this line EXCEPT
the three paragraphs that start SITE MARK:, EXCLUDE: and OUTPUT:, and send these
three paragraphs in their place, then your SUBJECT line:

    NO LETTERING: Draw no lettering of any kind: no text, letters, numbers,
    symbols, captions, labels, watermarks, signatures, site name, web address or
    copyright sign. Where the scene would normally show writing (a sign, a screen,
    a book, a badge), leave that surface blank or show plain shapes. A site mark
    is not your job: it is added to the finished file afterwards by other means.

    EXCLUDE: Company logos, product UI screenshots, flags and recognizable
    people. No photorealism, glossy 3D rendering, neon cyberpunk effects, busy
    circuitry or dramatic gradients. Do not depict a vendor feature as tested or
    proven when the article only reports an announcement.

    OUTPUT: A clean illustration with no lettering at all. A genuine JPEG exactly
    1600 x 900 pixels. Inspect the actual image before use and regenerate it if it
    contains any lettering, pseudo-text or logo. Automated file checks do not
    approve visual style.
