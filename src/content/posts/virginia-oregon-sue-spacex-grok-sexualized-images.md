Virginia and Oregon have filed separate lawsuits against SpaceX and its AI and social media units over Grok's image and video tools. [Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones](https://www.oag.state.va.us/media-center/news-releases/jay-jones/3162-attorneys-general-jones-and-rayfield-file-suit-against-spacex-parent-company-of-x-xai-and-grok-over-creation-and-promotion-of-child-exploitation-and-non-consensual-images-on-platform) and [Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield](https://www.doj.state.or.us/media-home/news-media-releases/ag-rayfield-takes-legal-action-against-grok-for-failing-to-block-child-exploitation/) announced the cases on 23 November. They allege the companies let Grok make sexualized images of real people without their consent, including children, while telling users that Grok and X were safe.

## What the states allege

Both cases are brought under consumer-protection law: the [Virginia complaint](https://www.oag.state.va.us/files/Grok-SpaceX-Complaint-As-Filed.pdf) under the Virginia Consumer Protection Act, in the Circuit Court for the City of Richmond, and the [Oregon complaint](https://doj.state.or.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/ODOJ-Grok-Complaint-Final-Submitted-2026-11-22.pdf) under the Unlawful Trade Practices Act, in Multnomah County Circuit Court. Oregon names SpaceX Super Intelligence Corp., SpaceX Super Intelligence LLC, X Corp. and Space Exploration Technologies Corp. SpaceX Super Intelligence is the new name of xAI.

The core of both complaints is a gap between words and output. The states say Grok was marketed with a "spicy" mode built to produce nudity, and that the companies' public statements about safeguards and a "zero tolerance" policy on child sexual exploitation were false and misleading.

The complaints lean on outside research. Virginia's release cites a Center for Countering Digital Harm estimate that Grok created and posted about three million sexualized images on X between 29 December 2025 and 8 January 2026, about 23,000 of which appeared to depict children. Oregon's complaint says that study worked from a random sample of 20,000 images, so these are estimates, not counts of illegal images.

Virginia asks for an injunction, restitution to consumers, disgorgement, and civil penalties of up to $2,500 for each willful violation. Oregon asks for a permanent injunction, disgorgement and civil penalties in an amount to be set at trial.

## SpaceX's response

Asked for comment, SpaceX Super Intelligence pointed to a statement on its X Safety account, [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/oregon-virginia-spacex-grok-deepfake-lawsuits.html) and [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/states-sue-spacex-to-stop-grok-from-generating-child-sex-images-revenge-porn/) report. It says the company acted to tighten Grok's protections, takes action against accounts that try to break its safeguards and notifies law enforcement, and that people who try to create this material should be prosecuted. None of the states' claims has been tested in court.

## Why this matters

If you ship image or video generation, note the legal route: the states are not using new AI law, they are comparing what a company said about safety with what its product did. Safety promises in your marketing, docs and policy pages can become the evidence. It is the same approach eight states took when they [sued OpenAI over ChatGPT's safety for young users](/posts/state-attorneys-general-sue-openai-chatgpt/), and it lands as [Apple's App Store rules now cover AI-made nonconsensual intimate images](/posts/apple-app-review-ai-nudify-bait-and-switch-rules/).
