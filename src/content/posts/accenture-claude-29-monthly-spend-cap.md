On 15 October 2026, Joseph Cox reported in [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/accenture-tells-employees-they-can-now-spend-only-29-on-claude-per-month/) that Accenture had told many employees the company will now spend $29 a month per person on Claude. The page's published time is 16:46 UTC. Cox writes from an internal announcement 404 Media says it saw, titled "Claude Enterprise Users: Important Updates to Licensing and Usage Costs." This story does not have the memo. Every figure below is 404 Media's account of that announcement and of internal messages it says it viewed.

## What 404 says the announcement sets

Cox writes that the announcement says the changes took effect on 1 October, and that they include centrally funding only $29 a month per person toward Claude usage. He quotes the announcement: "all costs are consumption-based." If someone goes past the $29, he writes, the rest has to be charged to a specific project or team. Accenture calls that bucket a Work Breakdown Structure, or WBS.

He also quotes this passage from the announcement:

> Remember: Claude Enterprise will continue to be available based on role and business need. Providing a WBS and obtaining a license does not guarantee indefinite or ongoing access to Claude. License allocations are reviewed periodically, and access may be reassessed based on business priorities, role requirements, usage patterns, and evolving workforce needs. As needs change, licenses may be reallocated to help ensure they are used where they provide the greatest value.

404 Media says Premium plans come with $250 of token usage a week, and that many employees are not on that plan. It says people already on Premium might be covered, and that people on what it calls the General plan, with usage already high, are now expected to find a way to fund that use. Those plan names and the $250 figure are Cox's report, not a sentence this story can quote from a memo it has not seen.

## What 404 says employees wrote

Cox describes three internal messages. In one, an employee says current usage costs exceed $500, and have in past months as well, and asks: "How can I speed up the upgrade to a premium account? The upgrade appears to be blocked on the support page. This obviously has a significant impact on project costs."

In another, an employee writes, "Honestly, this is disappointing," and says they use Claude Code "extensively" to build and test an "AI accelerator" being developed for multiple Accenture clients. Their monthly usage, they added, is usually around $500 to $1,000. The story's opening line says some employees who had been spending as much as $1,000 a month are looking for ways to keep that access. A third message, Cox writes, shows an employee whose Claude access was revoked altogether. He does not quote that message.

An Accenture spokesperson told 404 Media by email: "At the foundation, we make AI available to all our people. Our approach today is to provide the right tool for the right work, and we continue to invest in AI capabilities, skills and enablement that deliver greater value for clients and our own organization." The statement, as printed, does not mention $29, Claude Code, WBS codes, or a date.

## The public partnership, which is a different document

On 9 December 2025, [Accenture's newsroom](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2025/accenture-and-anthropic-launch-multi-year-partnership-to-drive-enterprise-ai-innovation-and-value-across-industries) and [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-accenture-partnership) announced an expanded partnership and an Accenture Anthropic Business Group. Both say approximately 30,000 Accenture professionals will be trained on Claude. Both say Claude Code will be made available to tens of thousands of Accenture developers. Those pages do not state a per-person monthly cap. They are the companies' account of the 2025 agreement, not a confirmation of the October funding change.

A [separate explainer here](https://aitamer.news/posts/cost-per-finished-task-not-per-token/) walks through why a token price and the cost of a finished task can diverge, using Anthropic's public price list. It is a measurement note. It is not evidence of what Accenture's memo says.

The $29 figure, the 1 October effective date, the WBS overage rule, and the $500 and $1,000 staff figures stand on 404 Media's reporting of documents and messages it says it saw. Accenture's on-the-record sentence is the one quoted above.
