## Update, 9 October 2026: Team year and $1,000 credits paused

Three days after expanding the program on 6 October, Anthropic paused the two headline offers. In a [post on 9 October](https://x.com/claudeai/status/2108404561413349695), the Claude account wrote: "Unfortunately, we underestimated demand for the program, and we need to pause the Claude Team and $1,000 API credit offers." It said Anthropic is re-reviewing applications, with "hundreds of thousands of applicants."

What that means depends on where an application stands, according to the same post:

- **Already claimed:** "If you've already claimed the Claude offers, they'll still remain in your account."
- **Applied, or approved but not claimed:** the application is being re-reviewed. "Unfortunately, this means that some accounts who had already been approved will not get access to the offer."
- **All accepted members:** they keep the Startup Stack and Applied AI office hours. Monthly API credits that come with a Claude Max or Team plan are "available in Console as usual."

The [program page](https://claude.com/programs/startups) FAQ now says Anthropic is "currently over capacity on the Claude Team and $1,000 API credit offers" and is "reshaping the Claude Startups program to ensure this initiative is of, by and for startup founders." It adds that the re-review "may result in a change in the status of some applications, which we regret." The page's top section now lists the Startup Stack and office hours, not the Team year or the $1,000 credit.

Sarah Wolf, who leads startup marketing at Anthropic, gave more detail in the [post the Claude account quoted](https://x.com/sarahzorah/status/2108401845563806150). She wrote that the program received "21x the number of applications" in its first 48 hours compared with the previous five months, and: "We didn't properly tune the acceptance criteria at the start, and many non-startups were accepted this time around." She said Anthropic is emailing everyone who has not claimed the benefits and will re-verify those applications.

The rest of this post describes the program as Anthropic's page and the coverage presented it on 6 October.

## The program as announced on 6 October

On 6 October, Anthropic's [Claude for Startups](https://claude.com/programs/startups) page said approved founders would get $1,000 in Claude API credits, a free year of Claude Team, and up to $45,000 in offer value from participating partners. The page said those benefits were subject to the [Startup Program Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/startup-program-official-terms).

The FAQ was more specific then. Claude Team was free for one year, for up to five Premium seats, and only if the organization was new to Team. A company that already had a Teams plan did not qualify. Every approved company was to get $1,000 in API credits, expiring six months after they were granted, on the first-party Claude API through Claude Console. The FAQ still says program credits cannot be used on AWS Bedrock, Google Cloud Vertex AI, or other third-party platforms.

The Claude Startup Stack, which continues, is discounts and credits the page values at up to $45,000, from independent third parties, redeemed in the Console. Venture funding is not required. A company backed by a VC in Anthropic's partner network may be eligible for up to $100,000 in additional API credits, claimed through that VC, the FAQ says. Members who receive program credits also get higher API rate limits.

## Who could apply, on the page and in the terms

The FAQ then said startups founded in the last five years, or funded in the last two, could apply. The applicant needed a Claude Console account, a company email matching the website's domain, and a short description of what the company was building. Anthropic said it reviews every application, and that applicants must comply with its supportability policies.

Applied AI office hours, which continue, are 45-minute live sessions every other week, with a registration link by email and in the portal.

The terms page says "Last updated May 22, 2025." Eligibility is Anthropic's sole discretion. A company is not eligible if it is established or operating in, or plans to serve people in, Belarus, China, Cuba, Iran, Myanmar, North Korea, Russia, Sudan, Syria, Crimea, or the so-called Donetsk or Luhansk People's Republics, or if export controls bar it. Anthropic employees and their immediate families and household members are not eligible. The "$1,000" in those terms is a liability cap (program benefits awarded, or $1,000, whichever is less). It is not the credit amount, which was set on the program page. Existing members do not reapply. The FAQ says they can redeem the console offers.

## What the coverage adds

[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/06/anthropic-gives-startups-a-free-year-of-enterprise-service-and-1000-in-token-credits/), in a 6 October story by Russell Brandom at 9:00 a.m. PDT, reports the same credits, seats, and office hours. It quotes an Anthropic announcement: "We created this program because we believe the benefits of AI will reach most people through the companies that build on top of models, rather than through the models alone."

[CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/06/anthropic-claude-startups-program.html), by Ashley Capoot at 12:00 p.m. EDT on Tuesday, says the program launched in May and that thousands of companies are already participating, according to Anthropic. Beth Robertson, head of startups, told CNBC: "Startups are often the first to push Claude to its limits." CNBC calls the stack, in Anthropic's words, a "set of benefits for the tools a startup runs on." CNBC's own framing is that Anthropic has spent much of the last year trying to fend off rivals such as OpenAI and Google. That rivalry line is CNBC's.
