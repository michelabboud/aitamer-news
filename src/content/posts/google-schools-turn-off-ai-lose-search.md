Google is changing the default for AI in schools. In a [3 November post](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/ai-learning-era-updates-2026/), Chris Phillips, Google's VP and general manager of education, wrote that Google is "updating our approach to controls for Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search. Access to these products will be entirely off for K-12 student accounts until school administrators explicitly turn them on."

The list includes Google Search, and that is the catch.

## Search counts as an AI product

Google's post does not explain why Search is on the list. Phillips did, in an interview with The New York Times that [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/google-will-let-schools-disable-its-ai-tools-but-not-without-sacrificing-search/) and [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1025411/google-turn-off-search-ai-features) quote: "Search and AI are deeply integrated." If a school restricts AI altogether, he told the paper, students "will not have access to search, and they'll see a message from their school that indicates it's not available."

In other words, the choice is per product, but Search arrives with its AI features attached. Google told [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2305432/google-says-that-students-in-schools-that-ban-ai-wont-have-access-to-search/) that administrators will be able to enable the products individually and that it is not all or nothing. The Verge reports that administrators will not get an option to toggle Gemini-powered features off without disabling Search.

## When it happens

Google has not given a date. The post says: "We'll share more specifics with schools in the coming months ahead of the changes rolling out so they have time to prepare and enable a smooth transition." The Verge notes the change comes months after Google turned its Gemini assistant on by default inside the Google Classroom app.

## What else Google announced

- **Guided Learning in Classroom:** available globally from 3 November. Google describes it as a study tool that coaches students step by step instead of giving answers, assigned by a teacher and anchored in class material.
- **Guided Learning in Search:** for student accounts, Search will walk students through certain homework questions instead of answering them directly. Google says a pilot starts in select U.S. schools later this month.
- **Funding:** a separate [Google.org post](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/google-org-funding-ai-education/) says it is providing "over $9.5 million in funding across five organizations building with and for educators", including CUNY, Fab AI, Teaching Lab and 1EdTech Global.

## Why this matters

For anyone building education tools, the planning assumption changes: on Google's K-12 student accounts, Gemini will start switched off, and each school decides what comes back. Google's position that AI is now part of Search itself is the same bundling French publishers [took to the EU](/posts/french-publishers-eu-complaint-google-ai-overviews-ai-mode/) over AI Overviews, and here schools get to opt in only by accepting it. It also adds to pressure on AI for minors, after New York [banned unsafe companion features](/posts/new-york-bans-unsafe-ai-companion-features-for-minors/) for under-18 users.
