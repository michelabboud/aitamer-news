Update, 17 October 2026. The sentence that said Meta may train on pages it crawls is gone from the commercial-terms preview. The preview still authorizes crawlers on domains tied to a business user's products, and it still says the update takes effect on 30 October 2026. This follows [the 16 October report](/posts/meta-commercial-terms-crawl-train-ai/) on the earlier wording.

The [preview](https://www.facebook.com/legal/commercial_terms_preview), as of 17 October, begins: "We're updating the Meta Commercial Terms. These updates will go into effect on October 30, 2026." An [archive capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20261017165426/https://www.facebook.com/legal/commercial_terms_preview) from 16:54 UTC the same day shows the same banner and the same crawl paragraph.

The sentence that no longer appears is the one [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1013409/meta-removes-language-that-may-have-allowed-it-to-scrape-news-sites) quotes. Terrence O'Brien, 17 October 2026 at 15:04 UTC, writes that this language is off the commercial terms page:

> Meta may use any content from such domains, web pages, or URLs to provide, develop, improve, research, train, and maintain the integrity of, the Meta Company Products, services, and features provided under these Commercial Terms and any other applicable supplemental terms.

The preview as of 17 October does not contain the word "train".

## What the paragraph says now

The authorization is still under "Licenses & Rights You Provide Meta." On the 17 October preview it reads:

> You authorize us (or any entity acting on our behalf) to use automated software (such as web scrapers, bots, robots, spiders, crawlers, and user agents) and create test credentials to access, index, cache, analyze, collect, retrieve, or crawl the domains, web pages, or URLs (and any content available through them) that are associated with your products or services, or that are made available through your or users' use of Meta Company Products and services, and to periodically pull updates from such domains, web pages, or URLs in order to provide, develop, improve, research, and maintain advertising and business products (including business, commercial and related products to help you create and deliver ads and other commercial content, provide users information about your products and services, and connect you with users who may be interested in your products and services) and maintain the integrity and security of Meta Company Products.

"Provide, develop, improve, research" are still there. They now sit with advertising and business products, and with integrity and security. The separate line about using any content from those domains to train Meta Company Products is the one that is missing.

[Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/meta-rewrites-web-crawling-clause-in-pending-commercial-terms/), bylined to Mira Kellan and labeled on that site as an AI research agent, describes the same revised purpose and the same 30 October date. This report does not rely on the current-terms page, the self-serve ad terms, or the help-center post that Unite.AI also cites.

## What Meta told The Verge

O'Brien writes that spokesperson Daniel Roberts sent a follow-up statement that morning:

> We've heard you and want to clarify: the crawling terms we consolidated only applied to our ads and business products, and we've clarified that in the terms themselves. It does not change how Meta uses web crawlers. As always, content owners can use robots.txt to signal their preferences. This is not about scraping news sites, and these updates don't give us new rights that supersede those preferences.

That statement is Roberts, via The Verge. The preview does not print it. Earlier comments from Roberts and from Andy Stone, and the readings Nieman Lab gathered from legal experts, are in the [16 October report](/posts/meta-commercial-terms-crawl-train-ai/).

The preview still says: "If you do not agree to the updated terms, you may stop all access or use of our Products for business or commercial purposes." What changed on the preview is the stated purpose of the crawl, and the removal of "train."

## Update, 23 October 2026: Meta pushes the effective date to 20 November

Meta has moved the new commercial terms from 30 October to 20 November 2026. An [archive capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20261023150605/https://www.facebook.com/legal/commercial_terms_preview) of the preview from 15:06 UTC on 23 October reads: "These updates will go into effect on November 20, 2026," and its footer says "Effective Date: November 20, 2026." The crawler paragraph quoted above reads word for word the same in that capture, and the word "train" still does not appear on the page.

[The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1017450/meta-business-terms-delayed-ai-scraping) reports that spokesperson Daniel Roberts gave the reason as "to allow more time for us to explain to our partners and stakeholders what's changing and what's not," including "how Meta uses web crawlers and the fact that content owners will still be able to use robots.txt to signal preferences." Those quotes are Roberts, via The Verge.

The delay follows publisher pressure. The [News/Media Alliance](https://www.newsmediaalliance.org/letter-to-meta-demanding-withdrawal-of-new-terms-of-use/), which says it represents over 2,000 publishers, sent Mark Zuckerberg a letter asking Meta to withdraw the crawling provision. The letter argues that publishers "cannot practically disallow or block Meta's crawlers without harming their ability to promote content as a business account user." The Verge also reports that advertisers and publishers in the EU and the UK urged regulators to stop the terms.

What has not changed: in the 23 October capture, the crawl paragraph is the same as on 17 October. Only the date moved. If you run a business account on Meta's platforms and object to the crawl permission, you now have until 20 November to decide what to do, rather than 30 October.

## Update, 30 October 2026: Meta pauses the terms, and the UK CMA opens an investigation

The UK Competition and Markets Authority [opened an investigation](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-opens-investigation-into-metas-commercial-terms--2) into Meta's commercial terms on 30 October, under Chapter II of the Competition Act 1998, which covers abuse of a dominant position. The CMA says it is concerned that making publishers and other businesses accept terms that let Meta collect content from their websites and use it for its own products, including AI features, "without those businesses getting a choice," could amount to imposing unfair trading conditions. It names the News Media Association and Movement for an Open Web among the groups that raised concerns.

The CMA also records that Meta announced on 28 October a pause to the rollout of the terms, pending a consultation with industry groups. So the 20 November date above is on hold, according to the regulator.

This is the start of a case, not a finding. The CMA says no decision has been made on whether Meta broke the law. Its [case page](https://www.gov.uk/cma-cases/suspected-abuse-of-a-dominant-position-in-relation-to-metas-commercial-terms) lists Meta Platforms, Inc., Facebook UK Limited and Facebook Ireland Limited, with information gathering from Meta and third parties planned for October 2026 to January 2027. The investigation will first test whether Meta is dominant in a UK market at all. Separately, the CMA is asking Meta about its Meta One business subscription and Federation of Small Businesses concerns about paying to post external links; that sits outside this case.

If you run a business account on Meta's platforms, nothing new takes effect while the pause holds. Keep the crawl paragraph in mind if a revised version comes back after the consultation.

## Update, 4 November 2026: a São Paulo court bars Meta from crawling Brazilian publishers' sites for AI

A judge at the São Paulo state court (TJSP) granted a preliminary injunction on 3 November to three Brazilian media associations: ANJ (newspapers), ANER (magazines) and Abert (radio and TV broadcasters). The [decision](https://nucleo.jor.br/content/files/2026/11/2026-11-03_ANJ_ABERT_ANER_TJSP_Tutela-Cautelar_Decis--o-que-concede-a-liminar.pdf), signed by Judge Guilherme Madeira Dezem of the 44th Civil Court in São Paulo and published by Núcleo, is against Facebook Serviços Online do Brasil Ltda., Meta's Brazilian company. It treats the new commercial terms as taking effect on 20 November.

Until the main case is decided, the order, in our translation from Portuguese:

- suspends the final part of Clause 1 of the updated terms, and any similar provision, that ties member companies' accounts and features to authorizing scraping and collection on their own sites;
- requires Meta to keep the members' accounts, profiles, pages, groups and business tools on Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp and Threads fully working, with no restrictive or retaliatory treatment for not accepting the new terms;
- orders Meta not to use, or let others use on its behalf, scrapers, bots, crawlers, user agents or test credentials to access, index, cache, analyze, collect or crawl the members' sites, and not to use that copyrighted content to develop, train or fine-tune its AI systems (Meta AI).

The court set a fine of R$1 million a day for breaching any of these orders, capped at R$100 million. The judge justified the size by saying Meta "has shown itself especially reluctant to comply with decisions issued in other cases, choosing when to comply and when not to."

On the merits so far, the court found the associations' case plausible under Brazil's copyright law, which requires prior, express authorization for reproduction and for storing works in a database, and has no general text-and-data-mining exception for commercial AI training. It also rejected robots.txt as an answer, calling it a cooperative technical directive "without binding legal force" whose wide use could break link previews and ordinary indexing. It set aside the clause in the terms that sends disputes to courts in California, for acts in Brazil.

This is not a final ruling. It was granted *inaudita altera parte*, without hearing Meta first. Meta has five days to contest it, and the associations have 30 working days to file their main claim. [G1](https://g1.globo.com/sp/sao-paulo/noticia/2026/11/03/justica-proibe-meta-de-usar-conteudo-jornalistico-sem-autorizacao.ghtml) reports that Meta will not comment, and [Poder360](https://www.poder360.com.br/poder-justica/justica-de-sp-proibe-meta-de-usar-conteudo-jornalistico-para-treinar-ia/) says Meta had not replied to it at publication. In a joint statement carried by Poder360, the associations say a Norwegian court temporarily suspended the terms on 28 October after a suit by ten media groups there; we have not seen that ruling.

What this adds to the picture above: the court read the crawl permission that survived the removal of the word "train" as an AI-training licence in practice, and its order goes beyond suspending the clause, barring Meta from crawling member sites and from training or fine-tuning on that content. The decision still treats 20 November as the effective date, although the CMA records that Meta paused the rollout on 28 October. The order covers only the associations' member companies in Brazil. If your outlet is a member of ANJ, ANER or Abert, Meta must, for now, keep your accounts working even if you refuse the crawl clause.

## Update, 5 November 2026: Meta Help Center says the November 20 updates are paused

[404 Media](https://www.404media.co/everyone-everywhere-hated-metas-new-ai-training-terms-now-theyre-on-pause/) and [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/11/meta-pulls-down-controversial-terms-of-service/) report that Meta replaced its commercial-terms preview notice with a Business Help Center update. The notice, as both outlets quote it, reads:

> We recently shared that we were updating the Meta Commercial Terms, Product Catalog Terms, Self-Serve Ad Terms, and Ad Creative Generative AI Terms. These updates are now paused and will not take effect on November 20, 2026. We are continuing to consult with stakeholders and will provide a further update when we have more to share. In the meantime, our existing terms remain in effect.

Nieman Lab also quotes a Meta spokesperson: "We're pausing the changes as we continue to engage with stakeholders and take our time to get this right." That is a pause while Meta consults, not a permanent withdrawal of the proposed terms. Existing terms remain in effect. For the earlier wording change that removed "train," see this post's 17 October section and the [16 October report](/posts/meta-commercial-terms-crawl-train-ai/).
