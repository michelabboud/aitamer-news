Shopify News (**2026-10-01**) introduces **Canvas**: a new **design surface** where merchants build with **Sidekick**, Shopify’s AI agent—framed as a whole-store visual workspace that is **rolling out to merchants over the coming days** ([Shopify News](https://www.shopify.com/news/introducing-canvas)).

This is a **theme / online-store design** harness—not Shopify’s **Checkout WebMCP** agent-checkout tools, and not Honeycomb’s similarly named Canvas product.

## Whole-store workspace, real theme code

Shopify describes Canvas as a place to see **every page laid out together**, **pan** across the store, and **zoom** into details. Merchants can chat with Sidekick and/or click elements; changes land in Canvas in real time ([Shopify News](https://www.shopify.com/news/introducing-canvas)).

The company stresses merchants are **not editing a static preview**, but a **render of the real code** behind the store—previewing interactivity and animation, and key pages with different products, collections, and screen sizes ([Shopify News](https://www.shopify.com/news/introducing-canvas)).

## Sidekick on theme files

On the agent side, Shopify says Sidekick works **directly on the theme’s files**, with a simplified theme architecture. Sidekick **validates code** and **reviews screenshots** in a refine loop before handing the result back to the merchant ([Shopify News](https://www.shopify.com/news/introducing-canvas)).

## Early rollout — not the editor replacement

Availability language is rollout-only: Canvas is **rolling out over the coming days**. Shopify has not claimed universal GA, all-plans availability, or mobile parity in this announce—and this brief invents none ([Shopify News](https://www.shopify.com/news/introducing-canvas)).

Ben Sehl, quoted in the same post: **“We’re early here and Canvas is not replacing the existing editor yet. Things will change quickly, and there are gaps we still need to close.”** Treat that as Shopify’s own maturity framing ([Shopify News](https://www.shopify.com/news/introducing-canvas)).

## Vendor color (Shopify-attributed)

Shopify says Sidekick has been **making more than twenty-five million edits to themes in the first half of 2026**—a **Shopify-attributed** history metric, not an independent audit ([Shopify News](https://www.shopify.com/news/introducing-canvas)).

Sehl also compares a past multi-week Kotn build to building a fully custom store in **“twenty minutes”** with Canvas—an **aspirational product quote**, not a measured SLA ([Shopify News](https://www.shopify.com/news/introducing-canvas)).

## Initial limits (TechCrunch)

TechCrunch’s Oct 1 coverage (Sarah Perez) reports Canvas is **desktop-only** at launch, and that **third-party theme support**, **app blocks and extensions**, **markets**, **translations**, **rollouts**, and **theme updates** are **not part of the initial launch**—with Shopify telling TechCrunch it plans to support those over time ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/01/shopify-debuts-canvas-a-way-to-build-online-stores-by-chatting-with-ai/)).

## Who should care

Merchants and theme teams watching Sidekick move from chat edits into a **whole-store design surface** should start at Shopify’s [Introducing Canvas](https://www.shopify.com/news/introducing-canvas) post. Lead with the **real theme-code render** and **theme-file edit loop**; keep **rolling out over the coming days** and **not replacing the existing editor yet** explicit; treat desktop-only and the TechCrunch gap list as launch limits, not a finished editor replacement.
