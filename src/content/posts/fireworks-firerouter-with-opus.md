Fireworks published **FireRouter with Opus** on **2026-09-28**—a **cache-aware** router optimized for the Opus family, available in the Fireworks **CLI** and, newly, as a **standalone serverless router model** any Fireworks account can point at “as you would any other model” ([Fireworks blog](https://fireworks.ai/blog/introducing-firerouter-with-opus)).

This is a **Fireworks Nexus coding-route** surface—not a multi-provider enterprise AI gateway.

## What it routes today

As of the announce, FireRouter with Opus routes among three **targets**:

- **Claude Opus 5.5**
- **GLM 5.3**
- **GLM 5.3 Flash**

Name them as **routing destinations** in Fireworks’ set—not as Fireworks-owned models or partnership announces. Fireworks says **the model set will change** as new models and versions launch ([Fireworks blog](https://fireworks.ai/blog/introducing-firerouter-with-opus)).

## How the cache-aware pass works

Each user turn is scored for how well each model in the set fits the task. The router then estimates cost—including **prompt-cache** cost and whether losing cache to switch models is worth it—and picks the quality/cost balance. Fireworks frames that prompt-cache awareness as the product’s differentiator; treat “first on the market” comparative lines as **vendor marketing**, not an independent ranking ([Fireworks blog](https://fireworks.ai/blog/introducing-firerouter-with-opus)).

## Internal A/B figures (Fireworks-attributed)

Fireworks reports more than a month of **internal** coding-traffic A/B (FireRouter with Opus vs Opus-only control):

- **98.1%** of Opus-only accuracy (78.7% vs 80.2% graded turns)
- **57%** lower session cost (**±19** percentage points), from **$15.36** to **$6.63** per session
- **94.2%** cache hit rate vs **97.8%** Opus-alone—described as a small, deliberate tradeoff

All of those numbers are **Fireworks internal results**, not independent benchmarks and not a public price card ([Fireworks blog](https://fireworks.ai/blog/introducing-firerouter-with-opus)).

## Surfaces and harness color

Beyond the CLI and the standalone serverless endpoint, Fireworks says FireRouter with Opus is available across harnesses including **Claude Code**, **Codex**, **Cursor IDE**, “and more.” Treat that list as **availability color**, not an exhaustive GA matrix ([Fireworks blog](https://fireworks.ai/blog/introducing-firerouter-with-opus)).

## Pricing (as stated)

The blog’s session-dollar pair (**$15.36 → $6.63**) comes from the internal A/B above. The post does **not** publish a public per-token or list-rate catalog for FireRouter, Opus, or GLM. This write-up invents none ([Fireworks blog](https://fireworks.ai/blog/introducing-firerouter-with-opus)).

## Who should care

Teams already on Fireworks who want a **cache-aware coding router** they can hit from the CLI or as a serverless endpoint—without standing up a separate multi-provider gateway—should start at the [FireRouter with Opus post](https://fireworks.ai/blog/introducing-firerouter-with-opus). Lead with the three current routing targets and the note that the set can change.
