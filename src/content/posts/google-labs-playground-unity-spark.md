Google is opening Playground, a browser tool for making games by describing them. The [7 October post](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/playground-experimental-gaming-platform/) by Maryam Karimzadehgan, a software engineer in AI innovation and research, calls it "an experimental gaming platform that lets you create, play, and share custom games." The post says it launches today "for users in the U.S. (18+) at playground.google, with tiered creation access rolling out based on your Google AI subscription."

Google's post does not say which models power Playground.

## What Google says you can do

Karimzadehgan writes that creation can be "as easy as describing it through a few prompts," with no coding experience required. The conversational interface, she writes, lets you "direct the game just by typing what you want to build." You can start from a blank canvas, remix starter prompts, or use guided support, and then test the game. Changes she names: tweak the physics, rewrite the rules, customize the characters, or overhaul the environment, by asking.

The games are browser-based, so the post says you can play them on a phone or a laptop. A finished game can stay private, go out as a shareable link, or be published to the Playground Explore gallery. "Select genres" support in-game leaderboards and multiplayer. A Play Games profile can carry a custom handle, likes, a leaderboard place, and follows. The gallery, Google says, uses player ratings and play activity to spotlight games. Every published game "undergoes safety screenings aligned with our Community Guidelines, along with user reporting." That screening claim is Google's.

## Unity Spark, and who is saying what

Google's post says Playground "will soon offer an integration with Unity Spark." In Google's words, Unity Spark is meant to add professional-level mechanics, high-fidelity 3D, and the Unity runtime, and "these games will be available for everyone using Playground to enjoy." The status line on the same page is specific: "Unity Spark is currently in testing, with closed beta coming soon." Google points readers to demo games on playground.google and to [unity.com/spark](https://unity.com/spark).

The Unity page title reads "Unity Spark: AI Game Maker with a Real Editor (closed beta)."

[The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1006477/google-playground-unity-spark-ai) says a closed beta for Spark is scheduled to kick off later this year, and that games made with Unity Spark will be shareable on Playground. [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/google-playground-and-unity-spark-will-enable-creators-to-easily-generate-3d-games/) also places a closed beta later this year, and it quotes Unity chief executive Matt Bromberg describing Spark as a way to make and share games in the browser with no prior Unity or game-development experience. Those "later this year" lines are the outlets'. Google's own sentence is "currently in testing, with closed beta coming soon."

The Verge adds a detail that is not in the blog post it quotes. It says Google spokesperson Nia Carter told The Verge that Playground is free to use, and that a Google One subscription raises weekly token limits by plan. Google's blog instead says "tiered creation access" based on a "Google AI subscription."

[9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/10/07/google-labs-playground/) tracks the same blog on prompts, private or public sharing, phone or laptop play, safety screening, and a U.S. rollout.

## What is actually open

Playground, on Google's account, is an early experiment for adults in the United States, at playground.google, with creation limits tied to a Google AI subscription. You describe a game, edit it in the chat, play it in the browser, and either keep it, share a link, or publish it. Unity Spark is the later step Google describes: in testing now, closed beta still ahead on Google's wording, and described by The Verge and GamesBeat as later this year.
