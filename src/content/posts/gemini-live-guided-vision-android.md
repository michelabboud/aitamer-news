Google's post on [1 October 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/guided-vision-gemini-live/) says Guided Vision is launching in Gemini Live on compatible Android phones. You share the camera during a Live session and hear descriptions, follow-up answers, and spoken cues to reframe the shot. Google says it was built with the blind and low-vision community, and that it had previewed the feature earlier in the month. The post's own limit is blunt: Guided Vision can be wrong, it is not a medical device, a mobility aid, or a white cane replacement, and it is not for navigation, safe-travel guidance, or obstacle detection.

## Who Google says shaped it

Google says it partnered with Aira "to visually interpret data for a total of tens of thousands of hours." More than 1,000 people in Aira's Trusted Tester network tried it on daily routines, and Aira specialists worked with Google on safety guardrails. The language section says descriptions and reframing cues reflect testing and feedback from blind and low-vision communities in India, Brazil, Singapore, Indonesia, Japan, "and more." The post does not list a language matrix. It also says the same feature can help older adults, people with low literacy, or anyone reading fine print in dim light. Those are Google's descriptions of the audience, not a clinical trial write-up.

## What a session is for, and how you start it

The examples on the page are close-range: reading a nutrition label, an appliance dial, or a menu; finding a dropped earbud or a jar in a cupboard; asking about color and pattern; getting a description of a table or an unfamiliar room. If the camera is too high, too close, or off to the side, Google says Gemini asks you to pan, tilt, or step back. That reframing is the feature's main interaction, and it is still a generative description.

To turn it on, the post says: in the Gemini app, open profile settings and enable "Use Guided Vision in Live," then start Live and share the camera. Android can also launch it from Settings, Accessibility, Vision assistance, Guided Vision, using the accessibility button, a two-finger swipe, or both volume keys. TalkBack users open the TalkBack menu with a three-finger tap and choose Guided Vision. It requires Android 9 or newer, in regions and languages where Gemini Live is already supported, with the Gemini app and the system software updated.

## Practical takeaway

This is a Gemini Live camera mode on Android, available where Live itself is available, with an accessibility shortcut and a TalkBack entry. Use it for labels, objects, and room descriptions. Keep a cane, a guide, or whatever mobility practice you already trust: Google says the model is not a substitute, and that it can make mistakes.
