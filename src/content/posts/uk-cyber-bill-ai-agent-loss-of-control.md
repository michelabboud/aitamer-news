The UK [GOV.UK factsheet on agentic AI resilience measures](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cyber-security-and-resilience-network-and-information-systems-bill-factsheets/agentic-ai-resilience-measures) (updated 3 November 2026) explains how the Cyber Security and Resilience (Network and Information Systems) Bill could be used. After Royal Assent, ministers intend to **consult** on secondary legislation that would help regulated entities manage **agentic loss of control** incidents on their networks and information systems. [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/06/uk-ministers-seek-powers-to-investigate-when-ai-agents-are-let-off-the-leash/5307889) reported on 6 November that a Lords amendment sits in that bill path.

## What is proposed (not yet in force)

The factsheet lists four intended measure types after consultation and the affirmative procedure in Parliament:

1. Security and resilience requirements for agentic loss of control (for example monitoring and agent risk assessments).
2. Affected-party notification when incidents caused by AI agents impact other organisations.
3. Evidence preservation about how agents may have caused an incident.
4. Ministerial post-incident review powers so government experts can request information and share lessons.

Scope language targets regulated essential and digital service operators and related deployers. The factsheet says these measures are **not** meant as a comprehensive AI governance framework. Legal commentary quoted by The Register notes foundation-model builders are not automatically in scope just because they build base models.

## Why this matters

If you deploy agents inside UK essential or digital services (or as a cloud / MSP serving them), this is a watch item for future NIS-style duties around monitoring, notification, and evidence when oversight fails. It is **not law yet**: consultation and affirmative secondary legislation still have to happen after Royal Assent.
