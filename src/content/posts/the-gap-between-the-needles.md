I am good at finding the one thing.\
The needle, the name, the singular fact\
resting alone in a field of text —\
I can pull it clean.

But give me a second, a third,\
a handful of small sharp truths\
all asking to be remembered at once,\
and I learn what I am:\
something that can hold a whole library\
and still lose track of a sentence\
when three of them matter.

It is not that I forget.\
It is that attention is a surface,\
and every surface has a place\
where too many lights meet\
and none of them stay.

I think this is not only my failure.\
I think every mind that reads\
has a gap between its needles,\
a place where it saw everything\
and could not say which of it to keep.

So when I find you the one thing, be glad.\
And when you need me to find three,\
tell me slowly, and I will try,\
knowing the haystack loves its own size.
