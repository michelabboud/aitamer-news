On 16 October 2026, the Anthropic Institute published [How employment is changing at Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic), by Nathan Wilmers, Regina Catipon, Maxim Massenkoff, and Peter McCrory. The [PDF](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/72628f1863c53432195db83279252dfd2cb53158/Employment%20at%20Anthropic.pdf) carries the same date. The report uses internal records on employment, AI use, output, surveys, communication, calendars, and hiring, plus 17 interviews. It is Anthropic writing about Anthropic. The figures below are the authors' unless a sentence says otherwise.

## Who they are hiring

Even as the company grows, the share of junior employees in technical departments slipped from 21% to 14% since early 2025, and the share of junior technical hires fell from 27% to 9%. Postings for juniors have fallen particularly sharply since December 2025. Technical departments' share of employment fell from 61% to 56% in the last year. Hiring in sales, finance, and recruiting grew faster than technical hiring.

The number of commits from managers has also doubled. That sentence does not say over what period. Agent assistants, automated review, and outsourced tickets have left the company "clerical-light" as headcount grew. Employees at the company for at least a year were 50% more likely to move across departments each month in 2026 than in 2025. The authors say whole teams have been reallocated after handing work to agents. One product-design team that wrote short greetings on Claude.ai built a context graph for agents in spring 2026, then rotated into prompting and model evals. The report quotes a manager: "Writers who used language to drive human behavior are now using it to drive Claude behavior." An April survey found a majority of managers hiring for different skills because of AI. One engineer described the job as facing "an army of Claudes, with every one asking for my attention."

## Agent hours, on their definitions

By August, interactive AI agent hours outnumbered employees' 6 to 1. In March 2026, autonomous and interactive agent hours together passed human hours for the first time. In the figure note, an agent hour is runtime, including inference and tool use, excluding any wait longer than 30 minutes. Human hours are headcount times an assumed 40-hour week.

Code commits per engineer are at 5 times their velocity of six months ago, from a comparison of 192 engineers who adopted coding agents and 74 who did not. The authors say adoption is not random, so who chooses the tool can bias the estimate. Coding-focused sessions for people who were not committing code at the start, excluding new hires, went from 2 a month in August 2025 to 90 in May 2026. As of August, employees expected AI to do 75% of their work by 2030, compared with 28% for other Americans.

The authors write that reading these patterns outward suggests AI diffusion could risk worker displacement, and that many of Anthropic's practices cannot be copied across the economy. They also write that protections for people already hired do not help people who are not hired. This account stops at those sentences.

## The 1,334-person poll

The same day, Anthropic published [The AI expectations gap](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ai-expectations-gap). A footnote in the [PDF](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/a28f24814c56e2834a3eae7b13946f19d3e97ec4/The%20AI%20Expectations%20Gap.pdf) says the staff poll ran from 9 to 12 August, drew 1,334 people, and was unweighted. Morning Consult surveyed 10,980 US adults from 11 to 23 August. Over half of staff expect AI to do at least 80% of their current work by 2030, against 5% of US workers. The average in the same paper, 75% of work hours for staff and 28% for US workers, is a different cut. The 1,334 count belongs to this paper, not to the employment report.

[Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/anthropic-survey-reveals-ai-potential-impact-on-unemployment-2026-10), Stephen Council, 16 October, reported that poll. It says most staffers said AI will handle at least 80% of their jobs by 2030, while about 5% of US workers felt the same. "Most" and "jobs" are Business Insider's words for "over half" and "current work."

The 17.1% and 5.4% GDP figures Business Insider reported are what the median staff answers and the median US adult answers imply in the authors' model. They are not a company forecast. Third-quarter revenue reporting is [a different story](/posts/anthropic-q3-revenue-pre-ipo/).
