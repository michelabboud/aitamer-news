Tidal says it will stop carrying music made entirely by AI. Its [AI Policy page](https://support.tidal.com/hc/en-us/articles/48031883413521-AI-Policy), updated on 24 November, now opens with a notice: "starting December 9, 2026, any wholly AI-generated track that's submitted to Tidal will be rejected and removed from our platform." Tidal's own account on X put it more bluntly: "Bye AI music. Starting December 9."

## Where Tidal draws the line

The policy defines AI-generated recordings as ones "wholly generated using generative artificial intelligence". Tidal says that, because of limits in AI detection and to avoid false positives, it will only act on wholly AI-generated recordings for now, and will extend action to "substantially AI-generated" ones as detection improves. It says it takes no action on AI-generated compositions, so a human performance of an AI-written song is not the target.

Until the change lands, the current rules still apply: Tidal labels recordings it determines are wholly AI-generated, those tracks earn no royalties, and listeners can switch off AI-labelled recordings in Settings so they stop playing and drop out of recommendations. Listeners can also report an untagged track through Tidal's support form.

## The numbers behind it

[Variety](https://variety.com/2026/music/news/tidal-bans-ai-generated-music-1236956924/) reports that Robert Andersen, the Tidal lead at parent company Block, wrote on X that 43% of tracks delivered to Tidal in the last 90 days were detected as AI-generated, that 96% of AI songs get zero plays, and that the move removes about 29 million tracks and means rejecting 80,000 to 85,000 songs a day. Those are Tidal's figures as relayed by Variety; we did not read the thread itself. Variety calls Tidal the first streaming platform to ban AI music outright.

Other services have stopped short of that. [Spotify said it pulled 50 million slop and spam tracks out of recommendations](/posts/spotify-ai-slop-tracks-recommendations/) this year, and [YouTube Music labels primarily AI-made tracks](/posts/youtube-music-ai-generated-labels/) rather than removing them.

## Why this matters

If you release music through a distributor, check how your releases are tagged before 9 December: wholly AI recordings will be rejected at Tidal, while AI-assisted ones and AI-written compositions stay in. Because enforcement rests on Tidal's own detector, expect disputes over what counts as "wholly" generated, and keep your project files as evidence of human work. For listeners, the Settings toggle already hides labelled tracks today.
