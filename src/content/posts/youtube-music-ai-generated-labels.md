On 14 October 2026, YouTube published [YouTube Music adds new labels for AI music](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-music-ai-labels/). The page's published-time metadata is 14:00 UTC that day. The post says that, starting that day, YouTube Music is rolling out labels so listeners can see when a song was created primarily using generative AI. The example it gives is using a generative model to create the composition and vocals. It says these music labels will also expand to the main YouTube app. The post gives no date for that expansion and names no countries.

## What YouTube says appears on the player

The post says that when AI played a significant role in a track, a label appears directly on the YouTube Music player page. Tapping the label opens a menu with more information. YouTube says it is teaming up with music partners and using its own detection tools, "applying the same labeling principles already used across YouTube." The blog post leaves the words on that label unnamed.

[The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1011369/youtube-music-ai-label), Stevie Bonifield, 18:22 UTC on 14 October, says the rollout starts that day in the YouTube Music app and expands to the main YouTube app later. It calls the player label "Made with AI" and says YouTube is adding it to tracks that were "altered or fully generated" using AI. Those phrases are The Verge's. The blog post uses neither one. The Verge also says that if a music partner makes no disclosure, YouTube may use other signals to decide whether a song is AI-generated. That part matches the Help Center page below.

## The three partner designations

YouTube's Help Center page, [Disclose Gen AI usage for music content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/17124251?hl=en), tells music partners to disclose generative AI so YouTube can label the media. It says YouTube may use additional signals to augment partner declarations. If a partner includes no Gen AI information, YouTube says it will rely on those signals and may designate the content as fully or partly Gen AI.

When partners do declare a value, the page's three affirmative designations are:

- Fully Gen AI: all content was created using generative AI.
- Partly Gen AI: part of the content was created using generative AI.
- No Gen AI: no content was created using generative AI.

If no value is provided, the page says YouTube will treat the field as unknown unless other signals support a determination.

Fully Gen AI, in the page's examples, is a track taken from an AI music generator, or a song whose composition, performance, and vocals were all generated. Partly Gen AI includes generating one layer, such as a bassline, and recording live vocals over it, or a human-performed song with an AI-made video. No Gen AI includes ordinary pitch correction, the page's example is Auto-Tune on small tuning mistakes, and automated mastering.

Partners can send that declaration through DDEX ERN 4.3.2 or later, or a CSV field `contains_ai` set to "all", "partly", "none", or "unknown". The page says "unknown" clears a bad declaration. YouTube Studio uploads can be marked in the attribute section.

[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/14/youtube-music-launches-new-labels-as-ai-generated-music-floods-streaming-platforms/), Lauren Forristal, 18:23 UTC on 14 October, reports the same three designations from that help page. It says the player label will also extend to the main YouTube app, and that clicking it gives more information about the specific role of AI in the track. The blog says the menu "shares more information." It does not say the menu spells out a specific role.

## Monetization, and what other services are reported to do

TechCrunch says AI involvement alone leaves a track eligible for monetization. The YouTube blog and the Help Center page state no monetization rule. That line is TechCrunch's report.

TechCrunch also describes other services. Those pages were not opened here. It says Apple Music plans "Made With AI" labels, as part of AI Transparency Tags, later this year; that Spotify has AI labels and a music spam filter; that Deezer said in July it was receiving nearly 90,000 fully AI-generated tracks a day, labels music it detects as AI-generated, keeps that music out of algorithmic and editorial playlists, and withholds royalties from fraudulent streams; and that TIDAL labels recordings it identifies as entirely AI-generated, lets listeners turn them off, and leaves them out of royalty attribution. The Verge says Spotify launched a voluntary AI label earlier this year and would not recommend songs from "AI personas," that Apple Music is reportedly getting an AI tag, that Deezer added a filter for AI tracks, and that Bandcamp banned AI music earlier this year.

The part YouTube itself states is the 14 October rollout on YouTube Music, the plan to expand the labels to the main YouTube app, and the partner scale of Fully Gen AI, Partly Gen AI, and No Gen AI, with YouTube's own signals when a partner leaves the field blank.
