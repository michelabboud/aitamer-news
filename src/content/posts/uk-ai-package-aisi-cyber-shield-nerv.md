Earlier today we covered the UK's new [AI Biosecurity Lab](/posts/uk-ai-biosecurity-lab-openai-anthropic/). It turns out to be one part of a larger package. HM Treasury [set it out on 24 October](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/chancellor-unveils-britains-ambitious-plan-for-ai) ahead of next week's Budget, under the line that AI should not "leave workers behind."

The Treasury does not print a total. Julian David, chief executive of the trade body techUK, is quoted on the same page calling it "a strong £600m+ package," and the priced items listed add up to about £620 million by our count, plus £10 million a year for jobs research.

## The security money

- **£165 million for a National AI Biosecurity Initiative.** £100 million of it is the AI Biosecurity Lab; the other £65 million goes to biosurveillance and medical countermeasures for priority pathogens. That second figure was not part of the lab announcement.
- **£120 million more for the AI Security Institute (AISI)**, the government body that tests advanced AI systems, for testing, research and technical standards.
- **£60 million for a National Cyber Shield** that will "use agentic AI" to improve UK cyber defences. It includes a £25 million challenge fund for UK AI and cyber security firms, and "scoping" for a regulatory sandbox for agentic cyber security tools in critical national infrastructure, the public sector and industry.
- **£70 million for a National Exposure and Real-Time Vulnerability (NERV) Centre** that will use AI tools to find vulnerabilities across critical infrastructure and the economy.
- A new **NCSC AI Cyber Security Centre of Excellence in Manchester** to train government cyber and AI staff. No figure is given for it.

## Industry, skills and jobs

- **£60 million more for the Sovereign AI Fund** to invest directly in UK AI companies, with a new focus on physical and industrial AI such as robotics and advanced manufacturing, plus **£60 million** for Sovereign AI's R&D procurement scheme, aimed at health and care and at AI security and assurance.
- **£18 million for a UK Advanced Chip Design Academy**; the page also mentions AI chip-design capability in Edinburgh.
- **£15 million for an AI Challenge Prize**, with the first award aimed at health.
- **£23 million over three years for the AI Economics Institute**, plus £10 million a year to study how AI will reshape jobs and industry.
- **£30 million for local AI partnerships** with mayoral strategic authorities.

## What is not on the page

Apart from the AI Economics Institute and the £10 million a year, the page gives no time span for any figure. It does not say whether the money is new or moved from existing budgets, who runs the NERV Centre, or what the Cyber Shield's agentic tools will be allowed to do on real networks. The sandbox is only being scoped. Expect those details in the Budget documents, if anywhere.

## Practical takeaway

If you build AI security or assurance tools in the UK, three doors open here: the £25 million Cyber Shield challenge fund, the Sovereign AI procurement scheme and, later, the sandbox. Watch for their calls. For everyone else, the useful signal is the split: about two thirds of the priced money goes to security, and the AI Security Institute gets a top-up as it keeps testing new models.
