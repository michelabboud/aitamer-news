A product dashboard says the run succeeded. A person affected by its result says something went wrong. The dashboard records what the system did. The person can describe what happened afterward. Both accounts matter when judging the outcome.

## The missing signal

The [NIST AI Risk Management Framework Playbook](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/playbook/measure/) says that some AI outcomes and impacts may be invisible to the people developing or deploying a system. Its Measure guidance calls for ways that end users and affected communities can report problems and appeal outcomes. It also calls for those reports to inform how the system is evaluated.

That makes a complaint more than a customer support message. It can reveal a gap in what the dashboard measures. A process that counts completed runs may have no field for a result that someone could not use, correct, or challenge.

## An appeal needs an answer

A reporting route should be easy to find from the place where someone sees the outcome. The person should be able to identify the decision, explain the problem, and submit relevant context without having to understand the model. They should receive confirmation that the report arrived, know who will review it, and learn the result of that review.

These are practical design choices, not guarantees that every appeal will succeed. An appeal can uphold the original outcome. It can also correct an error or expose a recurring problem. In each case, the person needs an answer they can understand.

## Reports should change the view

NIST suggests examining the types, rates, and results of appeals, as well as whether people know the feedback route exists. It also suggests measuring how well error reporting works. A team can use those signals to check whether its existing metrics miss harms that appear after deployment. The same guidance treats the ability to seek redress as part of transparency and accountability.

## What to do

Put a report and appeal link beside consequential outcomes. Give each report a reference number and a named review owner. Record the outcome and the time taken to respond. Review appeal patterns alongside dashboard metrics, then change the system or its measures when the reports show a gap. Finally, test whether affected people can find and use the route.
