Anthropic said on 18 October 2026 that it is committing part of its equity to a new entity, the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund. In its [announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/public-benefit-fund) the company writes: "Subject to stockholder approval, 5% of Anthropic's shares will be committed today, with an additional 2% added at the board's discretion for every 50% increase in the company's value above a set baseline."

So the 5% is not final until stockholders approve it, and the extra 2% steps are at the board's discretion, not automatic. Anthropic has not published the baseline value, and it put no dollar figure on the commitment.

## How it is set up

The fund is a company entity, not a charity. Anthropic says it is "establishing the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund as a wholly-owned LLC", choosing that "rather than a 501(c)(3) or other format" to give "adjustability between different forms of giving." It adds one firm limit: "None of the Anthropic equity held by the LLC will be used for lobbying or political giving, in the US or abroad."

Anthropic is a public benefit corporation. It says most of its mission work, such as safety and interpretability research, applied biomedical research and Claude discounts for nonprofits and scientific institutions, stays inside the company because it has "both a public benefit component and for-profit value." The fund is meant for things that do not fit there: "we might explore large donations to traditional nonprofits or direct distributions to specific populations."

## Labor is the named example

The one concrete area Anthropic names is the labor market. "AI may generate enormous economic value, but it could also cause instability for many workers," the post says. It points to an earlier [$200 million commitment](https://www.anthropic.com/news/economic-futures-research-fund-agenda) to study and address that, and says "given the pace of AI progress, bolder approaches may be needed. The Fund will have the versatility to pursue them." The post does not say whether "direct distributions to specific populations" means payments to displaced workers, and we do not read that into it.

Anthropic frames the escalating stake against a familiar practice: "It is common practice for tech companies to offer escalating compensation awards to their executives. We are instead directing that equity to the public benefit."

## The 20% figure

The post links the fund to earlier pledges. It says the co-founders pledged at founding to donate 80% of their equity, and that about half of employees take part in an equity donation match. "When combined with today's announcement, roughly 20% of Anthropic shares will be dedicated to the public good," it says, plus any milestone additions. That 20% is Anthropic's own sum of pledges and the new commitment. Pledged equity is not the same as equity already given away.

## What is not known yet

[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/18/anthropic-says-its-committing-5-of-its-shares-to-a-public-benefit-fund/) put the open question plainly: who "will actually decide which public benefits are worth funding." Anthropic says only that "in the coming months, we will share more about the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund's structure and governance, including the valuation milestones that may add to its stake," and that a public benefit report is coming.

Until then, these are unknown: who manages the LLC and answers for its grants, the baseline value for the 2% steps, when stockholders vote, and when, or how, shares turn into money for anyone outside the company. Because the LLC is wholly owned by Anthropic, the equity also stays inside the corporate group until it is given out.
