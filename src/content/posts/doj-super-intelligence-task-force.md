The US Department of Justice [announced](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-launches-super-intelligence-task-force/) the formation of its **Super Intelligence (SI) Task Force** (DOJ-SITF). "Super Intelligence" and "SI" are the administration's terms in the materials; this desk uses them only as quoted labels.

## Who leads it

Per the press release, the task force is led by Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward and Associate Deputy Attorney General Stephen Weiss. It is to work with the White House SI Task Force, whose chair the release names as Jay Clayton. Attorney General Todd Blanche is quoted on partnership with that White House body.

The linked [memorandum](https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1470291/dl?inline) (dated 13 October 2026) names Woodward and Weiss as co-chairs for the Attorney General or designees, and lists component seats including Antitrust, Civil, Criminal, FBI, National Fraud Enforcement, National Security, the Solicitor General's office and the Attorney General's Advisory Committee. It merges the Artificial Intelligence Task Force announced on 9 January 2026 into DOJ-SITF.

## What the PR and memo say it targets

The release says DOJ-SITF will work so that US leadership in SI is not compromised by misuse by foreign state and state-sponsored actors, transnational criminal organizations, and individual or corporate actors engaged in fraud or other unlawful conduct. The memorandum adds internal jobs: organise criminal and civil SI enforcement, deconflict matters across components and US Attorneys' Offices, recommend enforcement priorities, serve as the Department's contact with the White House SI Force, fold in affirmative civil litigation on SI, and develop guidance on prosecuting unlawful SI-related conduct and evaluating compliance measures.

Associate Deputy Attorney General Stephen Weiss is quoted on using law-enforcement tools to "deter improper use of SI, harden regular reporting on SI risk and response, and ensure companies exercise vigorous internal controls so that SI technology behaves as intended." That is his wording; the materials do not spell out new statutory reporting duties for companies.

The memorandum says it improves internal management and creates no enforceable right or benefit for any party.

## Why this matters

This is a coordination and priority-setting move inside DOJ, in the same policy lane as other government AI packages we have covered, such as the [UK AISI and cyber-shield funding note](/posts/uk-ai-package-aisi-cyber-shield-nerv/) and the [FTC civil investigative demands story](/posts/ftc-civil-investigative-demands-openai-anthropic/). Companies building frontier systems should read the Weiss quote as a signal that reporting, internal controls and fraud or national-security misuse are on the enforcement agenda, without inventing obligations the memo does not state. No charges, subpoenas or rulemakings are announced in these two documents.
