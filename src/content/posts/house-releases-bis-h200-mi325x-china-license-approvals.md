The House Foreign Affairs Committee [released export licence records on 19 November](https://foreignaffairs.house.gov/news/press-releases/chairman-brian-mast-exercises-legislative-authority-to-publicly-release-ai-chip-license-information-for-china) showing that the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) had approved licences for **more than 879,000 Nvidia and AMD AI chips** for Chinese companies. Committee chairman Brian Mast used his powers under the Export Control Reform Act to publish them and called it "the most detailed public disclosure in the history" of that law. The press release says companies had requested **3.4 million** chips in total.

## What the table shows

The [released table](https://democrats-foreignaffairs.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/11.19.26-ECRA-Information-Disclosure-of-BIS-License-Decisions.pdf) covers Nvidia **H200** licences "and equivalent" (AMD's MI325X) and shows status **as of 11 May 2026**, so it is a six-month-old snapshot. We added up the rows marked approved:

- **21 approved applications, 879,868 chips.** Nvidia accounts for 579,868 and AMD for 300,000 (four approvals of 75,000 each).
- **Every approved row says "Approved, not shipped"**, with China as the ultimate location.
- **Named recipients include** Alibaba (75,000), ByteDance through two entities (150,000), Tencent and a Tencent subsidiary (150,000), Baidu (43,000), JD (41,300), Kuaishou (75,000), Didi, miHoYo, VNET, Li Auto, BYD and Tesla's Shanghai unit, among others. One approved row lists Nvidia itself, for 8 chips.
- Other rows are marked Submitted, Rejected or RWA (returned without action) and are not in the approved total.

## The arguments

Mast's release highlights approvals for Alibaba and Tencent, which it calls "Department of War-designated Chinese Military Companies", and says the records include "requests by NVIDIA to depend on possible chip-smuggling fronts". Those are the chairman's characterizations. Per [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/nvidia-amd-china-chip-sales-congress.html), ranking Democrat Gregory Meeks said BIS "has failed to enforce the rules". A BIS spokesperson told CNBC licensing decisions involve "deliberation and consultation among multiple U.S. Government agencies". Nvidia told CNBC that approvals came with "strict conditions and limited volumes" and that "we have since stopped shipping H200s to China", and AMD said it complies with export rules.

## Why this matters

This is the first itemized public look at who the US was willing to license frontier-class Nvidia and AMD accelerators to, and it puts names and volumes on a policy usually argued in generalities. The records stop in May and show approvals, not deliveries, and Nvidia says it has stopped H200 shipments. If you plan capacity or compete with Chinese labs, treat this as a measure of licensing intent and watch the follow-up oversight, not as a count of installed GPUs.
