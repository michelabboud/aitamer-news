Two official guides were both open on 22 October 2026, and they do not describe the same first screen.

## Install, then sign in

The [Cursor get-started page](https://cursor.com/docs/grok-bot/get-started) sends you to the Grok Bot page of the Cursor dashboard. **Download Grok Bot** installs the desktop app or copies a link. Without dashboard access, ask an admin for the link. The page lists macOS (Apple silicon and Intel), Windows (x64 and Arm64), and Linux (x64 and Arm64), and says the app checks for updates. iPhone install is the App Store, on iOS 18 or later, with the same Cursor account.

The [SpaceXSI get-started page](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/get-started) sends you to the downloads page: an Apple silicon or Intel disk image, a Windows x64 or Arm64 installer, and Linux packages under **More downloads** on x.ai/bot, as `.deb`, `.rpm`, or AppImage. **Check for Updates** is under **Settings**, then **Updates**. Phone updates on the [how-to page](https://cursor.com/help/grok-bot/how-to) come from the App Store or Google Play. If desktop says **Update required**, that page is the recovery. A reinstall from cursor.com/dashboard/bot keeps the Bots and chats, because they belong to the account.

Choose **Sign in** and finish in the browser. There is no separate Grok Bot login. Single sign-on is the company Cursor flow. Already inside the app, the SpaceXSI page uses **Sign In with Cursor** under **Settings**. If access is a SuperGrok subscription, link it when asked. On iPhone and Android that page shows **Link Grok Account**, then **Finished Linking? Refresh My Status**. On desktop, **Settings**, then **Usage & Billing**, offers **Get Access with Grok**.

The Cursor page says access comes with every paid individual Cursor plan, the Cursor Teams plan, an individual SuperGrok link, or a seat on a self-serve Grok Business plan. The SpaceXSI "before you begin" list names the same Cursor plans plus SuperGrok, SuperGrok Plus, and SuperGrok Heavy, but no Grok Business seat, so it is the shorter list. [Episode 06](https://aitamer.news/posts/grok-bot-plans-pricing-usage/) is the matrix. Read it before you quote a price. This checklist prints none.

Grok Bot needs cloud data storage. Privacy Mode (Legacy) prompts you to switch before the app can start, and the [teams page](https://cursor.com/docs/grok-bot/teams) says that setting blocks Grok Bot entirely. [Episode 12](https://aitamer.news/posts/grok-bot-privacy-security/) is what leaves the computer after you are in.

## The first Bot, on the screen you have

The Cursor get-started page and [onboarding](https://cursor.com/help/grok-bot/onboarding) agree on the start. Click through the intro. The app creates a Bot named Grok Bot and opens its chat. Onboarding starts another Bot at **New chat**, the + button, then **Create new Bot**. The get-started page calls that + button **New Bot**. Type a name first and choose **Create "Name" Bot** if you want the name up front. On a team, **Create new Team Bot** in the command palette makes the shared Bot. Leave it for [episode 05](https://aitamer.news/posts/grok-bot-groups-teammates/) and episode 09.

[Edit a Bot](https://cursor.com/help/grok-bot/edit-bot) says personal Bots no longer have a description, and the details view no longer has a Bot settings page. Put the job in the first message and ask the Bot to remember it. Onboarding's example:

> Research one product topic at a time. Use the web and the documents I share. Summarize the main findings with source links, and ask before changing any external service. Remember this for future work.

Rename it, or add a label, from the name at the top of the chat. The phone calls the label **Title (optional)**. One job is the point.

The SpaceXSI page still opens on **Meet a future teammate**. It first asks which tools you use. Those answers only change the suggestions. They do not connect the tools. Pick a suggestion, or choose **Create your own**, and give a short name, one job, and a description. Later Bots there start at **New**, then **Create new Bot**. If that screen is showing, fill the three fields. If the screen is a chat with a Bot already named Grok Bot, use the first message. Episode 01 recorded the same split.

## One task that stops

A strong first request states the outcome, the sources, the constraint, the deliverable, and where the Bot stops. Attach a document. Onboarding's wording:

> Summarize this document in five bullets. List every date, decision, and open question in a separate section. Cite the section for each item. Do not change the source file.

Read the answer. Name any format you want again, in chat. Help will not offer a description field for it afterward. The edit-bot page says a lasting rule lives in the Bot's memory, and its example is "From now on, ask before sending any email." Episode 12 is why that memory line still does not press **Send** for you.

Connect Gmail, Calendar, Slack, or Notion from the connect card or from **Connect apps** when a later task needs them. [Episode 04](https://aitamer.news/posts/grok-bot-connectors-channels/) is the plugin. [Episode 11](https://aitamer.news/posts/grok-bot-gmail-and-sending/) is the draft you press. The first sitting needs neither.

On a site with no plugin, choose **Take over** or **Open computer**, enter the password or code yourself, and hand control back. The Bot does not see the password. The session stays on the shared computer, which is [episode 02](https://aitamer.news/posts/grok-bot-computer-the-box/). Use **Allow once** while you learn a workflow, and **Deny** when the action is not what you asked for.

Save a skill after a second run matches the shape you wanted. Schedule a routine only after that. [Episode 03](https://aitamer.news/posts/grok-bot-routines-scheduler/) is the clock. Teaching by demonstration, testing routines, and updating the computer stay on desktop even after the phone is signed in.

## A pass you can do today

Install from the page your account shows. Sign in with Cursor. On the screen you have, give one Bot one job and ask it to remember the stop. Attach a document you may share. Send the five-bullet summary, including the line that the source file stays unchanged.

Choose **Deny** on a card that would send, delete, purchase, or change a service. If a card wants a password, use **Take over** or the secure card. Stop there. The routine, the second mailbox, and the template are later episodes, and the index names which one.

That is the desk after thirteen episodes: one computer, one job with a remembered stop, a send you press yourself, and a list to reopen when the work grows a second Bot.
