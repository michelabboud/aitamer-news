Adobe and The Walt Disney Company have [announced a multi-year strategic alliance](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261109707511/en/Adobe-and-The-Walt-Disney-Company-Announce-Multi-Year-Strategic-Alliance-to-Support-Disney-Innovation-with-New-AI-Powered-Tools) that makes Adobe an "Official AI Innovation Partner" of Disney. At its centre is Firefly Foundry, Adobe's service for building custom generative models for a single company.

## What the deal covers

Adobe will give Disney early access to Firefly, Firefly Foundry, Premiere and Photoshop, plus dedicated engineering and research staff. Through Firefly Foundry, the two will build generative models "customized on Disney properties", trained on things like character attributes, environments and visual styles. [Reuters](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/business/disney-adobe-co-develop-custom-ai-models-tailored-media-giant-6536291) reports that the models will be private and proprietary to Disney.

The work starts in three places:

- **Disney Experiences**, through Walt Disney Imagineering, which designs the parks, resorts and cruise ships.
- **StudioLAB**, the innovation group inside Walt Disney Studios, for concept exploration.
- **ESPN Edge**, for sports storytelling and bringing more of ESPN's archive to fans.

Neither company disclosed financial terms.

## Already in use

This builds on a [June collaboration](https://news.adobe.com/news/downloads/pdfs/2026/06/06162026-adobexdisneyimagineering.pdf) with Imagineering. Two models from it are in use now, according to the release: a sketch-to-image model that turns hand-drawn concepts into rendered 2D concept art, and a custom image model that produces on-brand assets for specific franchises. The June announcement also described a 2D-to-3D prototyping capability; the new release does not say whether that has shipped.

Kyle Laughlin, Disney's senior vice president of R&D technology, told Reuters that the tools compress creative work "that would have taken months to now being able to do that in hours and days", and that they are "in no way meant to replace" Imagineers. Those are Disney's descriptions; neither company has published output samples or measurements.

## Why it is a different kind of model deal

Public image generators like the ones in our [image model series](/posts/ai-image-models-seedream-vs-qwen-image/) are trained broadly and sold to everyone. This is the opposite: a model tuned on one company's licensed catalog, kept in-house, and judged on whether it gets that company's characters and style exactly right. Adobe's June material made the case directly, saying models trained on scraped internet data "offer no guarantees around IP fidelity". That argument lands while publishers are taking AI companies to court over training data, as [USA Today Co. did with OpenAI](/posts/usa-today-sues-openai-copyright/) in October.

## Why this matters

If you build creative tools for brands, this is the template large rights holders are reaching for: a private model on their own assets, inside the editing tools their teams already use, rather than prompts sent to a public generator. It also signals that Firefly Foundry's selling point is provenance and brand accuracy, not raw image quality.
