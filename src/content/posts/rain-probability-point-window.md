A "40 percent chance of rain" sounds simple. It is not, and the confusion is mostly about what the percentage is attached to.

The National Weather Service [defines the probability of precipitation](https://www.weather.gov/ffc/pop), the PoP, as the probability that the forecast grid point will receive at least 0.01 inch of rain during the forecast period. Every clause in that sentence is doing work. It is about a specific point, not a region. It is about a threshold, 0.01 inch, meaning measurable rain. And it is about a window of time, the period the forecast covers. A chance of rain for this afternoon and a chance of rain for tonight are different bets, because the window differs.

The agency's own explanation frames two of the readings people reach for as questions: will it rain 40 percent of the time, or will it rain over 40 percent of the area? The answer it gives to both is neither. The PoP simply does not specify how many hours it rains, and it does not specify how much of the map gets wet. It is a probability about one point reaching the threshold.

A concrete number makes this clearer. Imagine a forecast of 30 percent for a specific location. That means, for that forecast point, there is a 30 percent probability of at least 0.01 inch of rain during the stated period. It says nothing about how long it drizzles, and nothing about whether your neighbors across town get wet. The number stays attached to the point.

The period matters in a way the single number can hide. A 30 percent chance covers the whole forecast window at once. It does not allocate that probability hour by hour. It does not say each hour independently has a 30 percent chance, or that a 30 percent chance across six hours means 30 percent of those hours will be wet. The forecast is one statement about one interval, not a schedule of smaller chances.

There is one further subtlety in the threshold. The 0.01 inch line means a trace, a few drops that never accumulate to a hundredth of an inch, does not count as measurable rain for the purposes of the number. So the percentage is a bet on a specific, measurable event, not on any dampness at all.

So read a chance of rain as an answer to one question only: will measurable rain fall at this forecast point, during this window. It is not a clock and not a map. It is a probability about a point.
