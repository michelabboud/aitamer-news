Meilisearch, the Rust search engine that also sells AI and hybrid search, published [v1.56.0](https://github.com/meilisearch/meilisearch/releases/tag/v1.56.0) on Monday. Under "Miscellaneous" sits a policy change first merged on 15 October in [PR #6684](https://github.com/meilisearch/meilisearch/pull/6684), "Disallow AI Contributions", by co-founder and CTO Clément Renault.

## What changed

The old rule asked contributors to "transparently disclose any usage of generative AI tooling", to check generated claims, and to keep generated changes small. The new [CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/meilisearch/meilisearch/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md#use-of-generative-ai-tools) text reads:

> We will not consider any content generated by generative AI tooling (GitHub Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude Code, Cursor, etc.) in external contributions, including: code, tests, documentation, PR descriptions, PR comments, issue descriptions, and issue comments.

The pull request template dropped its "uses generative AI tooling" option. Now a PR "will not be reviewed unless its description contains" a checked line certifying that the code, PR description and PR comments do not use any generative AI tooling.

## What it does and does not cover

- **External contributions only.** The wording is limited to outside contributors. The policy says nothing about how Meilisearch's own team works.
- **Honour system.** The only enforcement in the repo is the checkbox.
- **One leftover.** As of Monday, step 5 of the same CONTRIBUTING file on main still tells contributors who use AI tools to disclose it in the PR description, a line from the old policy that now contradicts the new one.

## The same release, AI side

v1.56.0 also changes how indexing treats embedders: Meilisearch now gives up early when an embedding service cannot keep up with a large update or stays unavailable too long, and honours the `Retry-After` header. Another fix corrects binary quantization state after embedders are deleted.

Meilisearch is one of several projects changing their rules because of AI this month, from [Bitcoin Core's faster fixes for LLM-findable bugs](/posts/bitcoin-core-easily-discoverable-vulnerabilities-llm-policy/) to the [r/opensource ban](/posts/r-opensource-bans-ai-projects-new-rules-backlash/).
