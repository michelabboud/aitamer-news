Graylark Technologies is selling police a geolocation tool that 404 Media says can take an interior photograph and point at a specific home. The [12 October 2026 story](https://www.404media.co/ai-company-lets-cops-pinpoint-specific-houses-based-on-a-single-interior-photo/) by Joseph Cox was published at 13:00:05 UTC. The product is Raven. 404 Media says it was previously called GeoSpy.

In a LinkedIn post earlier this month, chief executive Daniel Heinen announced "the ability to geolocate interior images across millions of short-term rental units worldwide," 404 Media reports. Another line 404 Media quotes from him: "In 2026, we're investing heavily in interior geolocation across U.S. hotels, short-term rentals, houses, and apartments." A further post, as 404 Media quotes it, says that for investigators in trafficking and exploitation cases "that connection can turn an unidentified room into a location lead they can verify and pursue."

## What 404 Media matched, and what it did not

404 Media describes vendor demo videos, not a test it designed. In one, a photo of a laundry pair is uploaded, the region is chosen, and the tool returns a result in Charlotte, North Carolina. 404 Media says the top result quotes a specific Airbnb listing there, and that the listing's photos show the same property. In another, a bathroom photo is matched to a Vrbo listing in Montecito, California. In a third, from a video posted last year, an interior photo is tied to a San Francisco property whose photos 404 Media says are the same as photos on Zillow. This story does not repeat listing titles or street addresses.

404 Media's distinction is the useful one. A reverse image search looks for the same image posted elsewhere. Raven, as 404 Media describes it, picks out features in the photograph and finds other photographs that appear to be the same property, including shots that are not identical and shots taken at a different time. 404 Media writes that it is unclear whether the marketing videos are representative of the tool's speed or accuracy.

404 Media also reports a city-specific angle. One video's interface includes the phrase "City-specific property AI to determine an address." 404 Media says that when it reported earlier this year that at least two agencies had bought access, emails showed Miami-Dade had a custom model trained for Miami-Dade County.

On where the interior behaviour came from, 404 Media recounts an interview Heinen gave Indicator last year. Heinen said the team had not trained the earlier product to do interiors, and that the behaviour showed up while testing a later version. That origin story is Heinen's, as 404 Media reports the interview. Indicator's own page was not opened.

## Who bought it, and what the platforms said

[404 Media reported on 12 February 2026](https://www.404media.co/cops-are-buying-geospy-ai-that-geolocates-photos-in-seconds/) that the Los Angeles Police Department and the Miami-Dade Sheriff's Office had bought access to GeoSpy. That story says the reporting rests on internal police emails 404 Media obtained. It is about the earlier exterior tool, under the GeoSpy name.

The October story quotes Joseph R. Peguero Rivera, of the sheriff's public affairs office, from an earlier exchange about that use: "To date, use of GeoSpy has been limited and largely exploratory. While it has been reviewed in a small number of cases, it has not resulted in any significant investigative breakthroughs or arrests. No case outcomes were driven solely or primarily by information generated by the tool."

Airbnb told 404 Media by email that protecting the community's privacy is important, that location metadata is excluded from listing photos, that hosts can show only an approximate location, and that a home's address is shared with a guest only after a reservation is confirmed. Airbnb said it has technical safeguards meant to limit automated collection. 404 Media writes that Airbnb stressed it has no relationship with Graylark, that its terms of service prohibit scraping, and that it is reviewing this case.

Zillow told 404 Media that scraping data is against its terms of use, and that when necessary it pursues legal avenues. Vrbo did not respond to multiple requests, 404 Media says. Those are the companies' statements as 404 Media reports them. They are not a court finding, and this story does not accuse Graylark of breaking the law.

In a September post on X, Heinen said Alexandr Wang, now head of AI at Meta, was an angel investor in Graylark, 404 Media reports. Wang did not respond to 404 Media's request for comment. The investor claim is Heinen's.

[404 Media's January 2025 story](https://www.404media.co/the-powerful-ai-tool-that-cops-or-stalkers-can-use-to-geolocate-photos-in-seconds/) describes GeoSpy when members of the public could still use it, including requests in a company Discord for help locating women. 404 Media says the founder pushed back on those requests, and that public access was closed, leaving government and enterprise users, the day after 404 Media contacted Graylark for comment.
