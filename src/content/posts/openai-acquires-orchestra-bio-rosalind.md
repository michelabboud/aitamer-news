OpenAI [says it has acquired Orchestra Bio](https://openai.com/index/openai-to-acquire-orchestra-bio/), "a platform that helps biopharma teams coordinate the complex process of drug development." The team and technology join Rosalind, OpenAI's models and tools for life sciences. The announcement, dated 20 October, gives no price or terms.

## What Orchestra does

OpenAI describes Orchestra as a platform "where people and AI agents work together to advance drug programs." Scientists and program leaders hand off monitoring and tracking of studies and campaigns to agents. OpenAI's example: a failed manufacturing batch can delay the start of a clinical trial, and Orchestra's agents work out which activities and timelines are affected, gather the information and compare responses. People then decide what to do.

That is operations software, not a discovery model. OpenAI notes that developing a medicine takes roughly 10 to 15 years on average in the US, and that much of the work is coordination across departments and partners "often relying on disconnected systems."

## Where it fits

Rosalind today is GPT-Rosalind, which helps scientists reason through biology and chemistry, and Rosalind Workbench, which connects it to scientific tools and data. We covered its [academic plan](/posts/openai-rosalind-academic-plan/) earlier. OpenAI says the combined aim is "agents that help teams connect scientific findings with the studies, decisions, and next steps needed to advance a potential medicine." The post mentions OpenAI's existing work with Novo Nordisk and Moderna but does not say they will use Orchestra.

There is no launch date, pricing or product name for the combined offering in the post. It lands the same week Anthropic published its [scorpion-toxin binder research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-scorpion-toxin-binder-design), so both large labs are now pitching life sciences directly, from different ends: Anthropic on design, OpenAI here on running the program.
