Meta has posted a preview of updated Commercial Terms that take effect on 30 October 2026. The [preview](https://www.facebook.com/legal/commercial_terms_preview), opened on 16 October, says the updates go into effect that day and prints "Effective Date: October 30, 2026." The text below is the preview.

Under "Licenses & Rights You Provide Meta," it says:

> You authorize us (or any entity acting on our behalf) to use automated software (such as web scrapers, bots, robots, spiders, crawlers, and user agents) and create test credentials to access, index, cache, analyze, collect, retrieve, or crawl the domains, web pages, or URLs (and any content available through them) that are associated with your products or services, or that are made available through your or users' use of Meta Company Products and services, and to periodically pull updates from such domains, web pages, or URLs. Meta may use any content from such domains, web pages, or URLs to provide, develop, improve, research, train, and maintain the integrity of, the Meta Company Products, services, and features provided under these Commercial Terms and any other applicable supplemental terms.

The preview says these terms apply to business or commercial use of Meta Company Products, except where Meta says separate terms apply. Its examples are ads or marketing messages, selling or listing products, developing apps, a product catalog, managing a Page, managing a Group for business purposes, a business product such as Ads Manager or Meta Business Suite, and measurement services. The crawl sentence says "associated with your products or services," and it also covers URLs made available through use of Meta's products. It does not say "news publisher."

## What experts told Nieman Lab

On 15 October 2026, Andrew Deck and Hanaa' Tameez reported in [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/metas-new-commercial-terms-will-allow-the-scraping-of-news-sites-legal-experts-say/) that the update will include news publishers with business accounts. The byline time is 12:59 p.m., with no time zone on the page. They describe those accounts as managing Facebook pages, Messenger, Instagram, or WhatsApp. Three legal experts reviewed the terms at Nieman's request. The article says that, as written, the terms could be used to argue Meta can scrape news sites and train on that content. That is their reading of the clause above.

Matt Topic, a media and intellectual property attorney at Loevy & Loevy, said: "This is a remarkable provision to bury in [the] terms of service, given that this would give Meta access to material that would likely cost many millions of dollars in a licensing market." The bracketed word is Nieman's. He added: "It’s not clear whether a court would enforce this, but publishers should assume that it would be enforceable."

James Grimmelmann, professor of digital and information law at Cornell Law School, said: "For news publishers, this clearly represents permission to Meta to train AI on any content from their sites and possibly to deploy some of that content, even in competition with the publishers themselves." He also said publishers "either have to trust that Meta doesn’t mean the full extent of what these terms say, or they have to stop [using business accounts]."

On "test credentials," Grimmelmann said the words would technically allow Meta to "sign up as a fake user" past a registration wall. Nieman Lab writes that Meta declined to say whether that would pass a paywall.

Benjamin Sobel, a law professor at the University of Wisconsin, called the pairing of business tools and crawl permission a form of "tying": "If you want to use the Meta products that require acceptance of the commercial terms, you have to also acquiesce to the scraping and crawling permissions exacted in those terms."

Nieman Lab says legal experts, unnamed in that sentence, see an opening to argue that the commercial terms supersede other blocking instructions. The preview does not mention robots.txt. The article also says training use would still turn on unsettled questions of AI training and fair use.

## What Meta has said

Nieman Lab says Meta declined a list of questions. An unnamed public-affairs spokesperson wrote that crawl language had been scattered across other terms, including generative-AI ad terms, and that this update consolidates it. He said, as Nieman quotes him, that the new terms will not change how Meta crawls publishers, and that "content owners can still manage how their content is accessed by using robots.txt."

[The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1012846/meta-business-terms-scrape-news-publishers), Emma Roth, 16 October 2026 at 14:02 UTC, names spokesperson Daniel Roberts. She writes that he calls the change "consolidating" the commercial and self-serve ad terms, and quotes: "There is no change to how Meta uses web crawlers; any suggestion to the contrary is false… Content owners can still use robots.txt flags to indicate their preferences, as we clearly explain in our developer documentation." Nieman Lab does not name its spokesperson.

Andy Stone, whose X profile says he works in communications at Meta, [posted](https://x.com/andymstone/status/2111101865769201850) at 14:28 UTC the same day. The text, read from the [fxtwitter status API](https://api.fxtwitter.com/status/2111101865769201850), is: "This is false. There's no change to how Meta uses web crawlers. As always, content owners can use robots.txt flags to indicate their preferences. This update is designed to make sure we’re able to pull accurate information about products for sale on third-party websites when making on-platform ad and content recommendations, not about scraping news sites."

## Disagreeing with the update

The preview says: "If you do not agree to the updated terms, you may stop all access or use of our Products for business or commercial purposes." Nieman Lab prints "please stop" where the preview says "you may stop." Nieman Lab says there is no opt-out that keeps the business products before 30 October. Stephen Beale, founder of The Steampunk Explorer, told Nieman Lab he saw the notice in Meta Business Suite and is considering closing his accounts before that date.

Nothing opened here says Meta has already crawled a site under these terms. The printed effective date is 30 October 2026. This report quotes the preview and the named comments. It is not legal advice.

Nieman Lab compares the terms with Meta One, a paid link limit it describes on some Facebook and Instagram accounts. The preview does not mention Meta One.
